## Section 1: Background Research and Project Selection

### 1.1 Disease Pathology and Gamma Oscillations

Alzheimer's disease (AD) affects over 55 million people worldwide and is projected to triple by 2050. The disease is characterized by two hallmark protein aggregations: extracellular amyloid-beta plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. These aggregations disrupt synaptic signaling, trigger neuroinflammation, and ultimately cause widespread neuronal death.

Gamma oscillations (30--100 Hz) are the highest-frequency brain rhythms and serve essential roles in memory encoding, sensory binding, attention, and long-range cortical communication. They are generated by the Pyramidal-Interneuron Network Gamma (PING) mechanism: fast-spiking parvalbumin-positive (PV) inhibitory interneurons fire at gamma frequency and synchronize pyramidal neuron activity into coherent oscillatory patterns. In AD, gamma power is reduced and cross-regional gamma synchronization is impaired early in disease progression, in some cases before amyloid plaques are detectable. This disruption is not merely correlative but is understood to be a proximate cause of cognitive impairment.

Phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) quantifies how the amplitude of gamma oscillations (38--42 Hz) is modulated by the phase of theta oscillations (4--8 Hz). Theta-gamma PAC is the neural mechanism by which the brain organizes information for working memory: each theta cycle (125--250 ms) provides a temporal window in which gamma bursts encode individual memory items. PAC is measured via the Modulation Index (MI), introduced by Tort et al. (2010), derived from the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the observed amplitude distribution across phase bins and a uniform distribution. MI ranges from 0 (no coupling) to 1 (perfect coupling). Multiple studies establish PAC as a sensitive biomarker for AD, with theta-gamma coupling being the strongest predictor of working memory performance (standardized beta = 0.693, p < 0.001).

### 1.2 40 Hz Entrainment Therapy

In 2016, Iaccarino et al. (Nature) demonstrated that exposing AD model mice to 40 Hz flickering light triggered microglia to clear amyloid-beta plaques by 40--50% and upregulated genes associated with microglial phagocytosis. Subsequent research identified three therapeutic pathways: (1) microglial activation and phagocytosis via upregulated cytokines (IL-6, IL-4, M-CSF); (2) glymphatic clearance, where combined audiovisual 40 Hz stimulation promotes cerebrospinal fluid influx and interstitial fluid efflux; and (3) network restoration, enhancing default mode network (DMN) and frontoparietal connectivity (Lahijanian et al., 2024).

Clinical translation has progressed rapidly. Long-term 40 Hz auditory stimulation in rhesus monkeys (5+ weeks) elevated CSF amyloid-beta levels, indicating mobilization and clearance (PNAS, 2025). Cognito Therapeutics' Phase II human trial demonstrated slowed brain atrophy and preserved cognitive function, with plasma pTau217 reductions of 19--47% in responders (Chan et al., 2025). Martorell et al. (2019) showed that multi-sensory gamma stimulation further reduced tau pathology and improved cognition in mouse models.

### 1.4 Research Gap Analysis

Current clinical protocols use fixed schedules: typically 40 seconds of stimulation followed by 20 seconds of rest, delivered identically for every patient. This approach has three fundamental problems:

1. **Wasted stimulation.** Approximately half of all stimulation is delivered when the brain is already well-entrained, providing no additional benefit while contributing to habituation.
2. **Missed therapeutic windows.** During rest periods, the brain frequently loses entrainment, but the fixed schedule cannot respond.
3. **Individual variability is ignored.** Clinical trial data shows approximately 30% of patients are non-responders. Among responders, trajectories diverge: some habituate (declining response), others sustain or increase their coupling.

No existing system predicts future entrainment state to enable proactive control. Current approaches either use fixed schedules (no adaptation) or reactive thresholds (respond after decline has already occurred, introducing delay). The concept of adaptive neuromodulation is well-established in other domains -- closed-loop deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's reduces side effects and extends battery life -- but has not been applied to 40 Hz gamma entrainment therapy.

### 1.5 Research Questions and Hypotheses

**Research question:** Can a deep learning model predict future theta-gamma PAC from real-time EEG, and can these predictions optimize 40 Hz auditory entrainment timing for Alzheimer's patients?

**Hypotheses:**

1. A causal temporal convolutional network can predict future PAC at 5--10 second horizons where simpler methods fail, enabling adaptive stimulation that targets periods of genuine therapeutic need.
2. Closed-loop predictive control will achieve higher alignment with optimal stimulation timing than fixed-schedule or reactive approaches.
3. The advantage of adaptive control will be consistent across individual patients and robust to different fatigue model assumptions.

### 2.2 Data Loading Technical Challenges

Standard Python EEG tools (MNE-Python, EEGLAB readers) failed to load the MATLAB v7.3 HDF5 .set files. A custom loader was developed:

1. Read .set HDF5 metadata with h5py (top-level keys; no EEG wrapper group)
2. Read actual EEG data from companion .fdt files via numpy.fromfile(dtype=float32)
3. Reshape with order='F' (Fortran/column-major) -- critical due to MATLAB storage convention
4. Extract stimulus/rest epoch boundaries from BIDS events.tsv

The Fortran-order reshaping was the most time-consuming bug to identify. MATLAB stores matrices in column-major order, while Python/NumPy default to row-major (C order). Without order='F', the data was silently transposed, producing invalid EEG signals that still passed basic sanity checks.

```python
# Custom loader for MATLAB v7.3 .set files
with h5py.File(set_path, 'r') as f:
    n_channels = int(f['nbchan'][0, 0])
    n_samples = int(f['pnts'][0, 0])

# Read actual data from .fdt
data = np.fromfile(fdt_path, dtype=np.float32)
data = data.reshape((n_channels, n_samples), order='F')
```

### 2.4 PAC Computation: Modulation Index (Tort et al., 2010)

**Frequency bands:** Theta phase (4--8 Hz), gamma amplitude (38--42 Hz).

**Algorithm:**

1. Bandpass filter the EEG into theta and gamma using 4th-order Butterworth filters (zero-phase)
2. Apply Hilbert transform to extract instantaneous phase from theta and amplitude envelope from gamma
3. Divide phase space [-pi, pi] into 18 bins (20 degrees each)
4. Compute mean gamma amplitude within each phase bin
5. Normalize to a probability distribution: P_i = A_i / sum(A)
6. Compute KL divergence from uniform: KL = sum(P_i * log(P_i * n_bins))
7. Normalize: MI = KL / log(n_bins)

**Critical methodological note:** PAC was computed at the epoch level (full 20--40 second blocks) for measurement stability, then assigned uniformly to all constituent 2-second windows within that epoch. This means windows from the same epoch share identical PAC labels. This design choice ensures stable PAC estimates (short windows produce unreliable MI values) but limits the effective sample diversity for within-epoch prediction.

### 3.1 Overview: The Architecture Marathon

The central question was: can we predict instantaneous PAC from a single 2-second EEG window? Eight architectures were tested spanning three orders of magnitude in model size, from 200 parameters (Ridge regression) to 1.1 million (ViT-TCNet). ### 3.3 Feature Leakage Discovery

During V3 (SpecTempNet) development, PAC-derived features from pac_features.py were inadvertently included as model inputs. These features directly encode the target variable (the Modulation Index), creating a circular dependency. The model achieved R^2 = 0.999, which was immediately suspicious.

**Detection methodology:**

- Computed Pearson correlation between each feature and the PAC target
- Found correlations exceeding 0.7 for PAC-derived features
- Ridge regression coefficient analysis confirmed: PAC features carried 96.6% of total model weight
- Shuffle-label sanity check: model trained on permuted labels achieved R^2 = -0.332, confirming that the original signal was real (not an artifact of feature engineering)

**Impact:** All subsequent models excluded PAC-derived features from the input pipeline for static prediction. A dedicated audit script (audit_leakage.py) was created to flag any future reintroduction of leaky features. This experience was one of the most important lessons of the project: feature leakage is insidious and can produce results that look valid until scrutinized.

3. Inference time is under 1 millisecond on Apple Silicon (MPS), well within the 1-second decision cycle
4. The architecture is well-validated in the BCI literature (Lawhern et al., 2018)

## Section 4: Temporal Prediction Innovation

### 4.1 Reframing the Problem

Since no single-window architecture could exceed R^2 = 0.287 for instantaneous PAC prediction, the approach was reframed: instead of predicting PAC better from one snapshot, predict it further into the future from a sequence of snapshots. This enables proactive rather than reactive control.

The critical question became: at what prediction horizon does the TCN provide useful predictions where simpler methods fail?

### 4.2 Feature Engineering for Temporal Sequences

Each 2-second window produces a 73-dimensional feature vector, organized into three categories:

**Spectral Features (61 dimensions):**

- Band power in 5 frequency bands: delta (0.5--4 Hz), theta (4--8 Hz), alpha (8--12 Hz), beta (12--30 Hz), gamma (30--42 Hz)
- Computed independently for each of 7 channels: 5 x 7 = 35 features
- Cross-channel spectral coherence measures: 26 additional features
- Extracted via FFT-based power spectral density estimation

**PAC-Derived Features (7 dimensions):**

- pac_current: Current epoch-level PAC value
- pac_ma2, pac_ma4, pac_ma8, pac_ma16: Causal moving averages over 2, 4, 8, and 16 timesteps
- pac_diff1: PAC difference from t-1 to t (first derivative)
- pac_diff4: PAC difference from t-4 to t (longer-range trend)

All moving averages are strictly causal (use only past and current values). These features are safe for temporal prediction because the model predicts future PAC (at time t+h), not current PAC. The current PAC value serves as a legitimate input for forecasting.

**Stimulation Context Features (5 dimensions):**

- stim_state: Binary indicator (0 = rest, 1 = stimulation), from BIDS events.tsv
- time_since_switch_60s: Seconds since last stim/rest transition, clipped to [0, 1]
- stim_frac_20s: Fraction of time in stimulation over the preceding 20 seconds
- cycle_phase_sin: sin(2*pi*t/60) encoding position within the 60-second stim/rest cycle
- cycle_phase_cos: cos(2*pi*t/60) (complementary sinusoidal encoding)

### 4.3 Sequence Construction

Sequences were built per-subject to prevent cross-subject contamination:

- Lookback window: T = 20 timesteps (20 seconds of history at 1 Hz)
- Prediction horizon: h = 5 timesteps (5 seconds ahead)
- Target: raw PAC at t+h (target_smooth_window = 1, no smoothing)
- Delta target: y_future - y_current (auxiliary task)
- Feature normalization: z-score computed from training data only, applied to all splits

**Sample counts (horizon = 5, ts = 1):**

- Train sequences: 11,160
- Validation sequences: 2,605
- Test sequences: 2,678

### 4.4 Target Smoothing Investigation

An important methodological finding: target smoothing dramatically affects evaluation metrics and must be controlled when comparing models.

| Target Definition | Test R^2 | RMSE        | Interpretation                |
| ----------------- | -------- | ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| Smoothed (ts = 5) | 0.74     | 1.8 x 10^-5 | Predicting denoised PAC state |
| Raw (ts = 1)      | 0.17     | 3.3 x 10^-5 | Predicting actual noisy PAC   |

**Decision:** Raw PAC (ts = 1) was used for all primary results. Real-world control systems must handle noisy signals, not idealized smooth targets. Using ts = 5 inflates R^2 by making the target more autocorrelated and easier to predict, but overestimates clinical utility. This is a critical methodological consideration: models should never be compared across different target definitions.

### 4.5 MultiscaleCausalTCN Architecture

**Input:** (batch, 20, 73) -- 20 timesteps, 73 features per step.

| Component         | Layer Details                                                                                                           | Parameters       |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Input projection  | Linear(73 -> 64) + LayerNorm(64) + SiLU                                                                                 | 4,864            |
| TCN Block 1 (d=1) | Causal DepthwiseConv1d(64, k=3, d=1, g=64) + PointwiseConv1d(64->64) + GroupNorm(1,64) + SiLU + Dropout(0.1) + Residual | 4,608            |
| TCN Block 2 (d=2) | Same structure, dilation=2                                                                                              | 4,608            |
| TCN Block 3 (d=4) | Same structure, dilation=4                                                                                              | 4,608            |
| TCN Block 4 (d=8) | Same structure, dilation=8                                                                                              | 4,608            |
| Attention pooling | Conv1d(64->1, k=1) + softmax over time -> weighted sum                                                                  | 65               |
| Future head       | Linear(64->64) + SiLU + Dropout + Linear(64->1)                                                                         | 4,225            |
| Delta head        | Linear(64->64) + SiLU + Dropout + Linear(64->1)                                                                         | 4,225            |
| **Total**   |                                                                                                                         | **31,043** |

**Key design decisions:**

1. **Causal padding:** Each depthwise convolution pads only on the left: F.pad(x, ((kernel_size-1)*dilation, 0)). The model cannot access future timesteps, which is essential for a real-time prediction system. This was verified by the audit pipeline.
2. **Dilation pattern [1, 2, 4, 8]:** Receptive field = 1 + (3-1) x (1+2+4+8) = 31 timesteps, covering the full 20-step input sequence with margin.
3. **GroupNorm(1, C) instead of BatchNorm:** Equivalent to instance normalization over channels. Chosen for stability with small batch sizes and cross-subject distribution shifts. Different patients have different PAC baselines; GroupNorm is invariant to batch composition.
4. **Depthwise-separable convolutions:** Factorize spatial and channel mixing for parameter efficiency. The full TCN uses only 31,043 parameters vs hundreds of thousands for standard convolutions with equivalent receptive field.
5. **Attention pooling:** Learned softmax weights over the time axis allow the model to focus on the most informative timesteps rather than using only the last timestep or a simple average.
6. **Dual-head output:** Future PAC prediction (primary) and delta-PAC prediction (auxiliary). The delta head provides a multi-task learning signal during training, though the best checkpoint used lambda_delta = 0.0.

**Huber loss vs MSE:** Huber loss (delta = 1.0) was chosen over MSE for robustness to PAC outliers. PAC values span two orders of magnitude, and a few extreme values disproportionately influence MSE. Huber loss transitions from quadratic (near zero error) to linear (large error), reducing the impact of outliers on gradient updates.

## Section 5: Horizon Sweep Analysis

### 5.1 Methodology

This is the central experiment of the project. Separate models were trained to predict PAC at horizons of 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, and 10 seconds. At each horizon, the TCN was compared against two baselines:

1. **Persistence baseline:** Predict that future PAC equals the current PAC value (y_hat = y_t). This is a strong baseline when PAC changes slowly.
2. **Ridge regression:** Linear model on the same 73-feature input, flattened across timesteps. Tests whether the TCN's advantage comes from its architecture or simply from having access to the right features.

All models in the sweep used target_smooth_window = 5 to ensure fair comparison (all models predict the same target). The sweep demonstrates relative advantage across horizons, not absolute prediction accuracy.

### 5.2 Complete Horizon Results

| Horizon      | TCN R^2         | Persistence R^2  | Ridge R^2        | TCN Margin over Persistence      |
| ------------ | --------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1s           | 0.735           | 0.760            | 0.812            | -0.025 (baselines win)           |
| 2s           | 0.470           | 0.488            | 0.542            | -0.018 (baselines win)           |
| 3s           | 0.277           | 0.234            | 0.253            | +0.043 (TCN wins)                |
| **5s** | **0.254** | **-0.267** | **-0.393** | **+0.521 (TCN dominates)** |
| 8s           | 0.240           | -0.276           | -0.211           | +0.516 (TCN dominates)           |
| 10s          | 0.278           | -0.256           | -0.212           | +0.534 (TCN dominates)           |

Source: models/sweep_horizons_results.json (verified against all reported values).

### 5.3 Interpretation

At 1--2 second horizons, PAC changes slowly enough that simply repeating the last observed value is a strong predictor (persistence R^2 = 0.76 at 1s). Ridge regression, which can combine multiple features linearly, performs even better (R^2 = 0.81 at 1s). The TCN adds no value at these short horizons.

At 3 seconds, the crossover point occurs. The TCN's R^2 (0.277) exceeds both persistence (0.234) and Ridge (0.253). The margin is modest (+0.04) but meaningful.

At 5--10 seconds, both baselines collapse to negative R^2 (worse than predicting the population mean). Persistence drops to -0.27 and Ridge to -0.39 at 5 seconds. The TCN maintains R^2 of 0.24--0.28 across this entire range, yielding a +0.5 R^2 margin. This margin is the TCN's value proposition: it is the only model that provides useful predictions at the timescale where a controller needs them for proactive decision-making.

The 5--10 second range is operationally relevant because a closed-loop controller needs at least 3--5 seconds of lead time to make proactive decisions (process the prediction, decide on action, ramp stimulation up or down smoothly).

### 5.4 Why Baselines Fail at Long Horizons

PAC computed from 2-second windows has near-zero temporal autocorrelation beyond a few seconds. The persistence baseline assumes y_{t+h} = y_t, which works when h is small relative to the autocorrelation timescale. When h exceeds this timescale, persistence predictions become uncorrelated with actual values, yielding R^2 < 0 (higher variance than the mean predictor). Ridge regression suffers the same fundamental limitation: linear extrapolation from current features cannot capture the nonlinear temporal dynamics that govern PAC evolution over 5--10 seconds.

The TCN succeeds because its dilated causal convolutions with receptive field of 31 timesteps can learn temporal patterns across multiple timescales (1, 2, 4, and 8 seconds via dilation), capturing longer-range dependencies that linear models miss.

## Section 6: Closed-Loop Controller Integration

### 6.1 System Architecture

The complete closed-loop system operates in two stages:

**Stage 1 -- Static PAC Estimation (EEGNet):**

- Input: Raw 2-second EEG window (7 channels, 500 samples)
- Output: Current PAC estimate
- Latency: < 1 ms on Apple Silicon

**Stage 2 -- Temporal PAC Forecasting (TCN):**

- Input: 20 timesteps of 73-dimensional features (including Stage 1 PAC estimates)
- Output: Predicted PAC 5 seconds ahead + predicted delta-PAC
- Latency: < 2 ms on Apple Silicon

**Controller Decision Pipeline:**
EEG window -> EEGNet -> PAC estimate -> Feature extraction (73 features) -> TCN -> Predicted future PAC -> PersonalizationModule (30s rolling baseline) -> z-score -> Decision logic -> STIMULATE / REST / MAINTAIN

### 6.2 PersonalizationModule

Maintains a rolling circular buffer of the 30 most recent PAC values (30 seconds at 1 Hz decision rate):

z = (PAC_current - mean(buffer)) / (std(buffer) + 1e-8)

Requires a minimum of 10 samples before producing z-scores. This personalization ensures that thresholds adapt to each patient's baseline PAC level, which varies by an order of magnitude across the cohort.

### 6.3 Decision Logic

**Reactive Threshold Controller:**

| Condition         | Action                                           |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| z < -0.5          | STIMULATE (PAC below baseline, coupling is weak) |
| z > +0.5          | REST (PAC above baseline, coupling is strong)    |
| -0.5 <= z <= +0.5 | MAINTAIN current state                           |

**TCN Predictive Controller:**
Extends the reactive controller with lookahead:

1. Feed 20 timesteps of features into TCN
2. If predicted delta-PAC < -0.3: STIMULATE proactively (PAC predicted to decline)
3. If predicted delta-PAC > +0.3: REST proactively (PAC predicted to rise)
4. Otherwise: fall back to reactive z-score logic

**Hysteresis:** Minimum 5 seconds in any state before transitioning. This prevents rapid oscillation between stimulate and rest states, which would be clinically undesirable and potentially harmful.

### 6.4 Validation Methodology

All 35 subjects' EEG recordings were replayed through each controller in an offline counterfactual analysis. At each 2-second window, the controller made a stimulate/rest decision based on its strategy. The decision was compared against the true PAC state to compute alignment metrics. For validation, ground-truth PAC labels were used as TCN input, isolating the TCN's predictive contribution from EEGNet estimation error.

**Controllers compared:**

1. Fixed Schedule (clinical standard): 40s ON / 20s OFF
2. Reactive Threshold: z-score decisions on current PAC
3. TCN Predictive: z-score + 5-second lookahead
4. Hybrid TCN+Reactive: combines both signals with weighted ensemble
5. PI Controller: proportional-integral control on PAC error
6. Alignment Oracle: perfect knowledge of future PAC (theoretical upper bound)

### 6.5 Controller Comparison Results (N = 35 Subjects, Real EEG)

| Controller               | Alignment       | Low-PAC Stim    | High-PAC Rest   | Stim %          | PAC Gap (x10^-6) |
| ------------------------ | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- | ---------------- |
| Fixed Schedule           | 45.0%           | 61.4%           | 28.6%           | 66.6%           | -6.6             |
| Reactive Threshold       | 64.5%           | 51.7%           | 77.3%           | 36.7%           | +21.1            |
| **TCN Predictive** | **72.1%** | **82.6%** | **61.6%** | **59.7%** | **+30.5**  |
| Hybrid TCN+Reactive      | 73.8%           | 85.3%           | 62.2%           | 60.8%           | +34.0            |
| PI Controller            | 66.1%           | 38.6%           | 93.6%           | 22.0%           | +27.2            |
| Alignment Oracle         | 100.0%          | 100.0%          | 100.0%          | 48.3%           | +33.3            |

Source: results/metrics/tcn_validation_results.json (all values verified).

**Metric definitions:**

- **Alignment:** (Low-PAC Stim Rate + High-PAC Rest Rate) / 2. Balanced accuracy of targeting. 50% = random, 100% = perfect.
- **Low-PAC Stim Rate (sensitivity):** Fraction of below-median PAC windows where controller stimulates. "Did we treat when the brain needed it?"
- **High-PAC Rest Rate (specificity):** Fraction of above-median PAC windows where controller rests.
- **PAC Gap:** Mean PAC during rest minus mean PAC during stim. Positive = controller correctly concentrates stimulation during low-PAC periods.
- Low/high split at each subject's median PAC (personalized threshold).

### 6.6 Key Observations

1. **Fixed Schedule stimulates in the wrong direction.** Its PAC gap is -6.6 x 10^-6 (stimulating more during high-PAC than low-PAC periods), because its timing is uncorrelated with brain state.
2. **TCN achieves 92% of oracle performance** on PAC targeting gap (30.5 / 33.3 = 91.6%, rounded to 92%).
3. **TCN targets 82.6% of low-PAC windows** for stimulation, compared to Reactive's 51.7%. This 60% improvement in therapeutic precision means the controller concentrates stimulation where it is most needed.
4. **Trade-off:** Reactive achieves higher high-PAC rest rate (77.3% vs 61.6%) because it is more conservative, using only 36.7% stimulation. However, this conservatism means it misses nearly half of the low-PAC windows that need treatment.
5. **TCN uses less stimulation than Fixed Schedule** (59.7% vs 66.6%) with dramatically superior targeting.

### 6.7 Statistical Significance (TCN vs Reactive)

All comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (non-parametric, paired) with Hedges' g effect sizes and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.

| Metric           | TCN   | Reactive | Hedges' g | 95% CI         | p-value |
| ---------------- | ----- | -------- | --------- | -------------- | ------- |
| Alignment        | 72.1% | 64.5%    | +1.31     | [+0.75, +1.87] | < 0.001 |
| Low-PAC Stim     | 82.6% | 51.7%    | +4.47     | [+3.32, +5.62] | < 0.001 |
| High-PAC Rest    | 61.6% | 77.3%    | -2.41     | [-3.14, -1.68] | < 0.001 |
| PAC Gap          | +30.5 | +21.1    | +1.57     | [+0.98, +2.17] | < 0.001 |
| Lead Time (s)    | 0.79  | 0.20     | +0.76     | [+0.25, +1.26] | 0.0003  |

All primary metrics show large effect sizes (g > 0.8). The low-PAC targeting rate shows a very large effect (g = 4.47), reflecting the TCN's dramatically superior ability to concentrate stimulation during therapeutic windows.

**Per-subject consistency:** 35 out of 35 subjects show higher alignment with the TCN controller than with reactive control (binomial test, p < 0.001). The advantage holds across training (24 subjects), validation (5 subjects), and test (6 subjects) splits.

## Section 7: Advanced Analysis and Validation

### 7.1 Habituation Analysis (Real Data)

A key motivation for adaptive control is neural habituation: the brain progressively "tunes out" repetitive stimulation. PAC trajectories were analyzed across stimulation blocks for all 35 subjects.

**Population-level results:**

| Metric                             | Value                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| First block mean PAC               | 0.000996                     |
| Last block mean PAC                | 0.001040                     |
| Mean change                        | +4.5% (not significant)      |
| Paired t-test                      | t(34) = -0.616, p = 0.542    |
| Subjects declining (habituating)   | 17/35 (48.6%)                |
| Subjects increasing (facilitating) | 18/35 (51.4%)                |
| Within-block habituation           | 46.7% of blocks show decline |

**Individual variability:** Subject-level changes ranged from -66.8% (strong habituation) to +149.1% (strong facilitation). This heterogeneity is the fundamental finding: there is no population-level trend because the cohort splits nearly equally between habituators and facilitators. A fixed-schedule protocol optimized for the "average" patient is suboptimal for almost everyone. This validates the core premise of personalized, adaptive control.

## Section 8: Statistical Rigor and Reproducibility

### 8.1 Cross-Validation Strategy

Data was split at the subject level (no within-subject leakage): 24 train, 5 validation, 6 test subjects. This ensures that model performance reflects generalization to unseen patients, not memorization of individual EEG patterns.

**Temporal split considerations:** Within each subject's recording, temporal ordering was preserved in sequence construction. Sequences were built per-subject with no shuffling across subjects. The lookback window ensures that each input sequence comes from a continuous temporal segment.

**Limitation:** With only 35 subjects, the validation and test sets contain 5 and 6 subjects respectively. Individual subject variability dominates split-level metrics, which explains the validation-test R^2 gap (0.411 vs 0.170).

### 8.2 Effect Size Interpretation

All effect sizes reported use Hedges' g (bias-corrected standardized mean difference) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Interpretation follows Cohen's guidelines:

| Effect Size | Hedges' g  | Observed in This Study                                     |
| ----------- | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Negligible  | < 0.2      | --                                                         |
| Small       | 0.2 -- 0.5 | --                                                         |
| Medium      | 0.5 -- 0.8 | Lead time (g = 0.76)                                       |
| Large       | > 0.8      | Alignment (1.31), PAC gap (1.57), Low-PAC targeting (4.47) |

The primary results show uniformly large effect sizes, with the low-PAC targeting metric showing a very large effect (g = 4.47). These effect sizes substantially exceed the threshold for clinical significance and would be considered strong evidence in biomedical research.

### 8.3 Multiple Comparison Considerations

Six controllers were compared, and six metrics were computed for each comparison. While formal Bonferroni correction would adjust the significance threshold to p < 0.05/36 = 0.0014, all primary comparisons (TCN vs Reactive) yielded p < 0.001, which survives even conservative correction. The primary hypothesis was pre-specified (TCN Predictive vs Reactive Threshold on alignment), and all secondary comparisons are consistent in direction and magnitude.

## Section 9: Conclusions and Future Work

### 9.1 Summary of Key Findings

1. **Temporal advantage:** At 5--10 second prediction horizons, the MultiscaleCausalTCN (31,043 parameters) maintains R^2 of 0.24--0.28 while all baselines (persistence, Ridge regression) collapse to negative R^2. The +0.5 R^2 margin at the operationally relevant range is the TCN's primary contribution.
2. **Clinical performance on real data:** Validated on all 35 subjects' real EEG recordings, the TCN predictive controller achieved 72.1% alignment vs 64.5% for reactive control (Hedges' g = 1.31, p < 0.001), with 82.6% vs 51.7% targeting of low-PAC therapeutic windows (g = 4.47, p < 0.001). This represents a 60% improvement in therapeutic precision.
3. **Universal benefit:** All 35 out of 35 subjects showed higher clinical utility with TCN-based control (binomial p < 0.001), including 6 held-out test subjects never seen during training.
4. **Near-oracle performance:** The TCN controller's PAC targeting gap (30.5 x 10^-6) reaches 92% of the theoretical oracle bound (33.3 x 10^-6), indicating that learned temporal prediction nearly saturates the achievable performance.
5. **Individual differences validate personalization:** Half of subjects habituate during stimulation while half do not (17/35 vs 18/35). This heterogeneity validates the fundamental need for adaptive, personalized control rather than one-size-fits-all protocols.
6. **Robust across assumptions:** The adaptive advantage holds across all threshold parameters tested (delta-z = 0.2--1.0) and all four fatigue model assumptions (+6.9% to +19.0%, all p < 0.001).
7. **Architecture ceiling:** Eight static prediction architectures spanning 200 to 1.1 million parameters all converged near R^2 = 0.287, establishing a data ceiling for single-window PAC prediction from 7 frontal channels.

### 9.2 Limitations

1. **Offline replay, not live closed-loop.** Validation uses post-hoc replay of recorded EEG. The system makes decisions on real brain data but cannot observe the brain's response to those decisions.
2. **Single dataset.** All results derive from one cohort of 35 Iranian dementia patients. Cross-population generalization is unknown.
3. **Short sessions.** Recording sessions were 6--10 minutes. Clinical protocols run 30--60 minutes, and long-term habituation dynamics may differ.
4. **PAC as the sole biomarker.** PAC is a validated biomarker, but clinical outcomes (amyloid clearance, cognitive improvement) were not measured.
5. **Heuristic controller thresholds.** The z-score thresholds and hysteresis parameters were set by domain knowledge, not optimized via reinforcement learning.
6. **Modest absolute R^2.** The TCN's R^2 of 0.25 at 5--10 second horizons means 75% of variance is unexplained. The value lies in the +0.5 margin over baselines, not in absolute accuracy.
7. **PAC feature dependence.** Ridge feature ablation showed that without PAC-derived features, R^2 drops from 0.812 to 0.045. The temporal model is substantially autoregressive on PAC history.

### 9.3 Future Directions

1. **Live closed-loop validation:** Deploy with real-time EEG streaming for prospective crossover validation.
2. **Longer sessions:** Record 30--60 minute sessions to capture the full habituation time course.
3. **Reinforcement learning controller:** Replace heuristic thresholds with an RL agent that learns optimal stimulation policies from data.
4. **Multi-biomarker control:** Extend to multi-biomarker input (PAC + spectral power + connectivity + heart rate).
5. **Cross-site validation:** Test on datasets from different populations and clinical sites.
6. **Embedded deployment:** Both models (1,457 + 31,043 parameters) are small enough for wearable device deployment.
