## Problem

Need a comprehensive, rubric-grounded understanding of how CSEF judges evaluate projects — specifically the Medicine & Physiology category. The existing Q&A bank was built from general knowledge, not from the actual CSEF judging rubric or category-specific criteria. Unknown: how deep do M&P judges go on statistics, clinical framing, model architecture, limitations? Do they test raw knowledge or evaluate scientific reasoning? Are there specific rubric dimensions (creativity, scientific thought, thoroughness, skill, clarity) that should shape how answers are structured?

## Solution

1. Pull the official CSEF judging rubric (Intel ISEF-aligned criteria) — covers Scientific Thought, Creative Ability, Thoroughness, Skill, Clarity of Presentation, and Dramatic Impact.
2. Find Medicine & Physiology category-specific judging guidance if available.
3. Map each rubric dimension to this project's strengths and likely probe questions.
4. Produce a document at `CSEF/presentation/interview/vfinal_judge_rubric_qa.md` with:
   - The actual rubric criteria and point weights
   - What M&P judges specifically look for vs general judges
   - Predicted hard questions per rubric dimension
   - Model answers for each, grounded in project results
   - Danger zones (questions that could expose weak spots)
5. Cross-reference with existing Q&A bank to fill gaps.
