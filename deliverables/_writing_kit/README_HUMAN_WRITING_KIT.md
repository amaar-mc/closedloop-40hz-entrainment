# Human Writing Kit — index and how to use it

**Built:** 2026-07-16. **For:** Amaar Chughtai, sole author, Valley Christian High School.
**Purpose:** get YOU writing YOUR OWN prose fast for every RED venue — venues that either
sign an anti-AI ethics statement (STS) or otherwise have no located policy permitting
AI-drafted text (JSHS, Synopsys, BMES HS Expo, SCCUR, AISES, AAN, BCI Award, BCI Meeting,
Sigma Xi, SfN late-breaking, plus the two expired-this-cycle venues). This kit contains
**no submittable sentences.** It contains questions, vetted facts, and fill-in-the-blank
scaffolds. You write every sentence that goes into an actual application.

---

## 1. Why this kit looks the way it does (read this before you write anything)

For STS specifically, you sign the Society for Science Ethics Statement certifying the
Research Report and application answers are your own composition. JSHS's Statement of
Outside Assistance requires disclosing any AI tool use in the research/writing process —
and the safest, simplest disclosure is "I didn't use it to write this." Most of the other
RED venues have no public AI policy at all, which this project's own governance treats as
**UNKNOWN → RED**, not as tacit permission.

So the tool that's actually useful here is not a draft. A draft would be evidence against
you at the exact venues that reward genuine, specific, first-person writing the most —
STS's holistic judges and JSHS's oral-defense panels are both explicitly listening for
whether the ideas sound like they came from the person who did the work. The fastest way
to write well under a deadline is not "generate then edit" — it's "have the facts, the
structure, and your own voice already staged so you're never starring at a blank page
deciding what goes where." That's what a question-based outline, an evidence packet, and
a fill-in-the-blank scaffold are for. They also happen to protect you: if a draft ever
surfaces after you certify sole authorship, that certification is false; if this kit
surfaces, it's exactly what it says it is — your prep material.

**Rule for this whole kit:** any example phrasing you see here is marked
`ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY — rewrite in your own words`, kept to a sentence fragment, and never a
sentence you could paste into a submission unchanged. If you ever find yourself copying a
fragment verbatim into a live application, stop — that defeats the point.

## 2. How to actually use this to write fast

1. Read `WRITE_IN_YOUR_OWN_VOICE.md` once, all the way through, before you touch any venue
   kit. It's short. It's the same primer for every RED venue.
2. Open the kit for the venue you're writing right now (`STS_RESEARCH_REPORT_WRITING_KIT.md`
   or `JSHS_PAPER_WRITING_KIT.md`).
3. For the section you're drafting, read the **questions** first — out loud if that helps —
   and answer them in a notes doc in your own words, roughly, before you touch the scaffold.
   The scaffold is there to catch you when you're stuck, not to be your first move.
4. Pull numbers from the **facts** table as you need them. Don't round, don't approximate,
   don't combine numbers from different experiments (the kits flag the ones that must never
   be paired).
5. When a paragraph feels done, run it against the self-check rubric at the bottom of
   `WRITE_IN_YOUR_OWN_VOICE.md`.
6. Keep every draft version and your notes. Version history is your own best evidence of
   authorship if anything is ever questioned later.

## 4. Shared self-check

Every RED-venue draft — regardless of which kit produced its scaffold — should pass the
self-check rubric at the end of `WRITE_IN_YOUR_OWN_VOICE.md` before you consider it done.
That rubric is the single shared quality gate across all thirteen RED venues.

## 5. Files in this kit

- `README_HUMAN_WRITING_KIT.md` — this file.
- `WRITE_IN_YOUR_OWN_VOICE.md` — voice primer, AI-tells-to-avoid, do/don't table, self-check rubric. Read first, applies everywhere.
- `STS_RESEARCH_REPORT_WRITING_KIT.md` — section-by-section questions, facts, and scaffolds for the STS Research Report (up to 20 pages).
- `JSHS_PAPER_WRITING_KIT.md` — same structure for a JSHS research paper, with the program-suspension notice up top.
