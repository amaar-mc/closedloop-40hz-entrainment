# QUESTION_BASED_OUTLINE.md — AISES 2026 Student Research Presentations

**How to use this:** answer every question below in your own words, in whatever order makes sense to you. Do not treat any bracketed text as a sentence to keep — it is a prompt, not a draft. Nothing here is submittable text; it is scaffolding so you write the actual abstract/poster yourself, fast. Any `ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY` fragment must be rewritten in your own words before it goes near a submission.

**Do not skip Section 0.** Everything after it is conditional on how you answer it.

---

## Section 0 — The gate question (answer this before anything else)

0.1. Do you have a genuine, honest connection to Indigenous community, culture, or identity — personal, family, geographic, or through a real intended application of this work to a specific underserved or tribal-health context — that you could truthfully write into a "situate yourself" section?

0.2. If you're unsure, try finishing this sentence honestly, out loud, to yourself: "My connection to Indigenous community impact in this project is \_\_\_\_." If what comes out feels like a stretch, or like you're searching for an angle rather than reporting something true, that is itself the answer.

0.3. If the honest answer to 0.1/0.2 is no — stop here. Tell the team "no genuine connection, do not pursue AISES" and this workbench is done. If yes — go to Section 1, but keep it as specific and true as what you just said, not a broader claim than that.

---

## Section 1 — Situating yourself (ONLY if Section 0 gate passed)

1.1. In plain terms, what is your specific, real connection to Indigenous community impact for this project? (Not "STEM research helps everyone" — something concrete and true to you.)

1.2. How does this project's actual output — a PAC-forecasting model and a retrospective controller replay for dementia-related EEG stimulation — connect to that real thing, without you inventing a bigger claim than the evidence supports?

1.3. AISES's Four Strands rubric scores "Interest in STEM," "Engagement & Belonging," "STEM Competencies & Confidence," and "Future Intentions." Where in your own research story do each of these actually show up, honestly? (E.g., a moment you were genuinely curious, a moment you felt you belonged or didn't, a specific competency you built, what you actually intend to do next.)

---

## Section 2 — The personal stake (independent of Section 0/1 — this is standard across your venues)

2.1. In one or two calm sentences, in your own words, what is the personal reason you started this project?

2.2. What did you actually understand about the problem before you started, and what surprised you once you got into the data?

---

## Section 3 — What you built and why (draw facts from EVIDENCE_PACKET.md, write the sentences yourself)

3.1. State in your own words: what does the static PAC model do, and what does an R² of 0.287 mean to someone who isn't a machine-learning person? Why is that a real finding rather than a disappointing one?

3.2. State in your own words: what is the difference between "reacting after the brain has already lost sync" and "forecasting before it happens," and why does that distinction matter for a stimulation system?

3.3. Describe, in your own voice, the moment you found that dropping 61 spectral features _improved_ your held-out result instead of hurting it. What did you think when you first saw that? What's your best honest guess at why it happened — and how do you make clear to a reader that it's a guess, not a proven mechanism?

3.4. Describe, in your own words, the SpecTempNet/circular-feature leakage story (or whichever leakage discovery you want to feature) — what tipped you off that something was wrong, and what you did about it.

---

## Section 4 — The honest limits (do not skip — this is often what wins with a rubric like Four Strands)

4.1. In your own words, why is your controller result a tradeoff rather than a win? (Fill in your own numbers from EVIDENCE_PACKET.md §2 Stage C — do not just copy the sentence there.)

4.2. What happens to your forecasting number under a stricter, leakage-free target definition, and why do you think it's important to show that number even though it's much lower?

4.3. What is the single most important thing a reader should NOT conclude from this poster? (State the boundary yourself — clinical claims, live deployment, etc.)

---

## Section 5 — What's next (Four Strands: "Future Intentions")

5.1. What do you actually intend to do with this project next — another experiment, a different dataset, a different application? Be specific and be honest about scope (a high-school researcher's realistic next step, not a grand claim).
