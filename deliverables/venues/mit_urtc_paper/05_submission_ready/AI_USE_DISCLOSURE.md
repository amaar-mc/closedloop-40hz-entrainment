## Statement

I used AI tools in two ways during this project, and I want both to be transparent.

**1. Software engineering.** During the build, I used AI coding assistants as a programming aid — for example, to help debug code, look up library usage, and speed up routine implementation. I treated this the way I would treat a reference textbook or a teaching assistant's help: the tools accelerated writing and checking code, but I designed the pipeline, chose the model architectures and features, decided the experiments, and wrote and verified the analysis. Every result reported in this paper was produced by code I understand and can explain, run on the OpenNeuro ds005048 dataset.

**2. Drafting this text.** I used an AI writing assistant to help draft and revise the prose of this manuscript. I directed the content, supplied every number and its source, checked each claim against my own results, and made the final wording and structural decisions. The scientific claims, their boundaries, the limitations, and the interpretation are my own.

**What AI did not do.** AI tools did not design the study, run or interpret the analysis, generate or alter any data or result, or decide what this paper claims. The numbers in this manuscript come from my own experiments and trace to a fixed set of canonical result files. All citations were verified by hand against the original sources.

## Notes for the author before submitting

- If the portal offers structured AI-disclosure checkboxes (IEEE has been adding these), tick the categories that match: _AI-assisted code development_ and _AI-assisted writing/editing_. Do not tick categories that imply AI-generated data, results, or analysis — none of those apply.
- Keep your version history, lab notebook, and result files as evidence of authorship. AI-text detectors are unreliable and can false-flag genuine student writing; a clear record of your process is your best protection.
