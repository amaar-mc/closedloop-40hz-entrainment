## Judging Criteria (40 Points Total)

| Category | Points | What Judges Evaluate |
|----------|--------|---------------------|
| Scientific Thought | 10 | Problem significance, clear hypothesis, controlled variables, sufficient literature, conclusions justified by data |
| Creativity | 10 | Originality of topic or approach, novel methodology, student's direct contribution |
| Independent Work / Skill | 10 | Student's execution, software competency, appropriate acknowledgment of help |
| Thoroughness / Clarity | 10 | Data adequacy, replication, documentation completeness, clear communication |

---

## Display Board Rules

- Max size: 48" wide x 15" deep x 60" tall
- Min font: 24pt body, title readable from 6 feet
- Required: Background, Hypothesis/Goal, Methods, Results (graphs not tables), Conclusions, References
- Prohibited: School names/logos, QR codes, contact info, institutional logos
- Abstract: Printed on TABLE, not board (max 250 words)
- Target: 300-500 words total on board

---

## ML/AI Project Requirements (2026 New Rules)

1. Clearly traceable data sources with citations
2. Unique insights (not just replicating existing work)
3. Design plan for model parameter development
4. Validation on DIFFERENT data than training
5. Test at least 2 of: Accuracy, Generalizability, Interpretability, Fairness, Scalability

---

## Notebook Requirements

- MANDATORY for all participants (no notebook = no awards)
- Paper or digital, must be present on Check-in Day and Fair Day
- Upload 4 pages to Judging Folder (first page, 2 middle pages, last page)
- Must show: chronological entries with dates, raw data, problems encountered, iterations, references

---

## Interview Format

- Judge preview (12:30-1:30): Judges review boards WITHOUT students
- Interviews (1:30-4:30): ~8-10 min per project
- 1-2 minute verbal summary (no slides, no reading from board)
- Then judge Q&A
- Judges review notebook during interview
- Software demos expected for computational projects
