## 2. Application components
- "The application consists of essay questions, questions about your project, a maximum 20-page original scientific paper, recommendations, transcripts and optional test scores." (FAQ)
- There are three recommendation types: the Educator Recommendation, the Project Recommendation and the High School Report (transcript). (REQ)
- Tasks in the application (RULES PDF): Task 5 Disclosures ("disclose support of any kind ... from paid support, AI use and any pre-existing relationships"), Task 8 Research Report Upload ("Upload a 20-page original research paper"), Task 9 Previous Research, Task 10 Essay Questions, Task 11 Activities (up to 7), and Task 12 Test Scores (optional).
- Essays (AQ, Task 10):
  - (1) "Your Potential as a Scientist, Mathematician or Engineer (maximum 200 words)".
  - (2) "Greatest Accomplishment or Challenge" (Common App prompts A/B/C, max 200 words; "we prefer that you think beyond your research project").
- Project questions (AQ):
  - "Layperson's Summary" (max 200 words).
  - "How did you get the idea ... What % credit can you take for the idea?" (250 words).
  - Duration (75 words).
  - Independence from a larger project (150 words).
  - "12. What Did You Do?" has six parts of 200 words each: purpose, design, implementation, data gathering, analysis and conclusions. The form says: "This is the bulk of the application where you can tell us what you did".
  - "13. What didn't you do?" (150 words).
  - "14. Describe any limitations" (200 words).
  - "15. Project Benefits and Impact" (200 words).
- Data provenance (AQ, Task 7): "E. How did you get the data used in your research report? Private? Online? Self-collected? Please describe. (125 words max)".
- AI reviewer (AQ): "4. Students are increasingly incorporating AI methodologies across many fields of STEM. Should an expert in Artificial Intelligence also review your entry?"
- Title format (AQ): "For any symbols, please write the name of the symbol in all capital letters (ALPHA, GAMMA, etc.) Otherwise, please use the AP-style Title Case." A "Short Title (100 characters or less)" is also required.
- Project recommender questions (PREC):
  - Q11: "For what aspects of the research can you give credit to the student as being their own unique contribution: Research Question, Procedural Design, Data Collection, Data Analysis, Drawing Conclusions (200 words each)."
  - Q13: "What did the student do that showed creativity and ingenuity? ... Were they creative in their science, or creative for a high school student?"
  - Q14: "would you hire this student again ... How do they rank against other students".
- The educator recommender ranks the student on "Independence, Creativity, Problem-Solving Abilities & Leadership Potential" (EREC).

## 3. Research Report format (RRG; same text is Appendix 2 of the RULES PDF)
- "The Research Report is evidence of research ability, scientific originality and creative thinking." (RRG)
- "Students must have completed an independent scientific investigation and have results to report. Research proposals, investigations not yet completed, literature reviews and essays are not eligible". "Students may not submit additional research after the application deadline." (RRG)
- Page limit: "The paper should be 20 pages or less. There is no page minimum. Pages of content beyond page 20 ... will not be read". The title page, abstract and bibliography do not count toward the limit. "Appendices count toward the 20-page limit." (RRG)
- Recommended structure: a "short introduction ..., an experimental design section including methods and results, and concluding discussion". "We do not require a specific format/order". (RRG)
- Formatting: text at least as large as "Times New Roman 11pt font", "1.5 line spacing and 1" margins", "Do not use multiple columns", page numbers bottom right after the abstract, and "PDF files that are 4MB or smaller". The file must be named "LASTNAME.FIRSTNAME.ZIPCODE". No links except in the bibliography. (RRG)
- AI in the paper:
  - "The Student Researcher is required to write the paper without the use of generative AI (ChatGPT or other programs)." (RRG item 1)
  - "Entrants should generate their reference lists without the use of AI ... Discovery of a fake reference will result in disqualification." (RRG)
- Figures: "Every single image, graph, table, chart, etc. that appears in the Research Report must be cited ... This includes images created by the Student Researcher. Failure to cite an image could result in disqualification." (RRG item 2)
  - Example credit line: "Graph created by the student researcher using BioRender, 2024." (CIT)
  - "Reference lists are not permitted for graphics." (CIT)
- Voice: "it is acceptable for a student to use the first person 'I' in place of 'we' ... This will help to clarify what was done independently vs. with support." (RRG)
- "Do not include library research or a history of literature beyond the short introduction". (RRG)
- Mentors and help: "full disclosure of any research or person that has influenced the applicant's work is required". "Adults reviewing research reports should suggest areas for improvement, but not provide the student with replacement text". (RRG)

## 4. Judging / selection
- Selection is holistic. "the research project, while important, is not the only factor for award decisions." (RULES PDF, Selection Process)
- "all portions of every eligible submission are evaluated by three or more doctoral scientists, mathematicians, and/or engineers in the appropriate scientific discipline." (RULES PDF)
- "Entries are evaluated in four areas: Research Report and Scientific Merit; Student Contribution to the Research; Academic Aptitude and Achievement; Overall Potential as a Future Leader of the Scientific Community" (RULES PDF)
- "Scholars and finalists are selected by the judges, using all available entry evidence, with greatest weight given to the Research Report. Judges are looking for students exhibiting exceptional research skills, a commitment to academics and their extracurricular pursuits, innovative thinking, promise as a scientist, and evidence of leadership in their communities. Regeneron STS does not share rubrics." (FAQ)
- "Regeneron STS is NOT looking for perfection, specific AP scores, ... participation at well-known labs, quotas or cut-offs of any sort." (FAQ)
- "Evaluators consider student circumstances and access to labs, activities and other personal contexts". (RULES PDF)
- Finals: "project judging, relating to their research, and panel judging, designed to evaluate the depth and breadth of their general scientific knowledge." (RULES PDF, FAQ)
- How the finalist announcements describe selection:
  - 2020, 2021 and 2022: "based on their projects' scientific rigor and their potential to become world-changing scientists and leaders" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2020-finalists/ ; https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2021-finalists/ ; https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2022-finalists/)
  - 2023 through 2026: "based on the originality and creativity of their scientific research, as well as their achievement and leadership both inside and outside of the classroom" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2023-finalists/ ... https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2026-finalists/)
- Entrant counts: 2,162 in 2024, 2,471 in 2025 and 2,612 in 2026 (finalist pages above).

## 5. Independence, mentors, public data
- "only independent, individual research is eligible." (FAQ) Team projects are ineligible and cannot be split. (RULES PDF)
- "Research conducted alongside adult researchers in a research institution is permitted, but clarity and adequate acknowledgment of an individual's role and independence versus work being done by the collective laboratory throughout the application is vital." (FAQ; RULES PDF)
- "Judges understand that sometimes scientists cannot allow students to choose their own topic or sometimes the main part of a procedure is already known. Students should consider what portions of the research they can take credit for". (FAQ)
- Research can be done "at school, at a research institution, in the field, or at home. Frequently this research spans a year or more, but some students have completed research in as short as six weeks." (FAQ)
- Public data is exempt from IRB: "Studies in which the data or tissue samples are preexisting and publicly available through public databases or published peer-reviewed research are exempt. In these circumstances, the student researcher will be asked to provide ... reference to the publicly available database". (RULES PDF, Human Data exemptions)
- Human testing needs IRB approval first. This is listed as a fail-to-qualify reason: "Student fails to obtain IRB approval before testing an invention, software or product, or conducting a survey of their design, or collecting feedback from other humans." (FTQ)
  - **[INFERENCE]** Any live test of the closed-loop stimulator on people, including friends or family, would need IRB approval obtained before testing. Offline replay on OpenNeuro data avoids this.
- Medical limits (RULES PDF):
  - "Students are prohibited from providing diagnostic or medical information to participants without direct supervision ... This includes publishing diagnostic apps on public websites or app stores without appropriate FDA approvals."
  - "Students are prohibited from using generative AI to diagnose or address medical issues, including mental health."
  - "Student project diagnoses or treats medical conditions" is a fail-to-qualify reason (FTQ).
- Published work: this is allowed, but the Society recommends submitting it only if the student is first or sole author (FAQ; RRG item 6). The AQ asks about "Open-Source Pre-Prints (arXiv)".
- Top 3 fail-to-qualify reasons in 2026 (FTQ): "Fake references and/or citations in Research Report"; "Vertebrate animal research conducted in a home environment"; "Failure to disclose a conflict of interest". The FTQ also lists "Unrefuted AI use in application responses".

## 6. AI rules (verbatim)
- RULES PDF, Entry Rules: "Students may not use generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) to write Regeneron STS application questions, draft the Research Report or generate citations. Students are responsible for personally drafting all responses to application questions. Use of AI tools for student research projects is permitted, and should be disclosed and appropriately cited. See acceptable AI usage chart on page 34."
- RULES PDF: "The presentation of fraudulent data, the evidence of plagiarism or the inappropriate use of AI are prohibited and grounds for the project to fail to qualify."
- Ethics Statement (RULES PDF), which both the student and mentor attest to:
  - "I attest that my Research Report and images within are properly credited and cited, and that I did not use AI tools to draft the paper or responses to application questions."
  - "I certify that I have not used AI tools, like ChatGPT, to construct the research report or responses to questions in the application."
- FAQ: "Use of AI tools for student research projects is permitted, and should be disclosed. Students may not use generative AI to write Regeneron STS application questions, draft the Research Report or generate citations."
- Plagiarism screening "authenticates essays, research reports and recommendation letters." (RULES PDF)
- AQ Disclosures:
  - Q7: "Is there any reason your Research Report might get flagged in a plagiarism or AI detector? Matches can occur when methods are common protocols, or AI was used within the project."
  - Q8: "Select all of the ways AI tools were utilized". The options are: Literature Search, Bibliography Support, Data Analysis, Project Ideation/Brainstorming, Graphic Design, Drafting Research Report, Editing/Refining Research Report, Writing Code, Translation, Other.
  - Q9: "list the specific tools ... and describe how they were used (maximum 200 words)".
- AI Usage Chart (AI chart; titled "Regeneron Science Talent Search 2027", adapted from the American Historical Association guidance of July 29, 2025):
  - "Use AI to write initial code for your project": "Acceptable, only with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts."
  - "Use AI to help identify appropriate statistical tests or software tools. (Interpretation of data must be done by the student researcher)": "Acceptable, requires a log of your prompts as part of your research notebook."
  - "Use an AI chatbot as a writing tool to help generate and develop ideas": "Acceptable, may require explicit citation ... Maintain a logbook of your prompts".
  - "Use generative AI to initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster": "Never acceptable. This must be the independent work of the student. Guidance or refinement after the initial document has been completed can be done with explicit citation and a log."
  - "You write an abstract. Ask AI to sharpen the language ...": acceptable "only if changes suggested by AI are minor and limited to grammar and syntax. Must be credited."
  - "Ask AI to add additional points to your research paper" is "Never acceptable". So are "Use AI to produce your conclusions, future steps" and "Ask generative AI to produce a starter bibliography".
  - "Ask generative AI to fix the structure or formatting of your bibliography": "Acceptable ... You must review and verify all citations as valid."
  - AI images: "Image should be clearly marked as AI-generated and with explicit citation".
  - "Ask generative AI to identify or summarize key points in an article before you read it": acceptable without citation. Reproducing an AI summary without reading the source is "Never acceptable".
- **[INFERENCE]** The RRG line "required to write the paper without the use of generative AI" is stricter than the chart's "refinement after the initial document ... with explicit citation and a log". The safe reading is that no AI touches the report prose beyond disclosed grammar fixes. Code written with AI help must be marked in the code and the prompts logged.

## 7. Categories (CAT; verbatim header)
- "Entrants must select one category from the list below; this will determine the expertise of the initial review only. Scholars and finalists are selected without regard to the category, and winners may not be selected proportionally across categories. During the review process, evaluators are able to request additional expertise from readers in other categories or suggest category reassignment as appropriate for the project."
- The list: Animal Sciences; Behavioral Sciences; Biochemistry; Bioengineering; Cellular and Molecular Biology; Chemistry; Computational Biology and Bioinformatics; Computational Humanities; Computer Science; Earth and Planetary Science; Engineering; Environmental Science; Genomics; Materials Science; Mathematics; Medicine and Health; Neuroscience; Physics; Plant Sciences; Social Sciences; Space Science.
- Neuroscience: "Studies of the neural basis of cognitive processes, including learning and memory, language and thought, perception, attention, and affect. It investigates the human brain, from the functional organization of large scale cerebral systems to microscopic neurochemical processes."
- Computational Biology and Bioinformatics: "Studies that primarily focus on the discipline and techniques of computer science and mathematics as they relate to biological systems. This includes the development and application of data-analytical and theoretical methods, mathematical modeling and computational simulation techniques to the study of biological, behavioral, and social systems."
- Medicine and Health: "Study of diseases and health of humans and animals – pharmacology, physiology, pathology, ... speech and hearing ...".
- Bioengineering: "Engineering principles applied to biology or medicine, such as bodily aids or replacements, medical/diagnostic devices, and drugs or other therapies using engineering to address a biological problem."
- Behavioral Sciences: "... cognitive processes ... and the biological bases of behavior."
- Categories used by precedent projects:
  - 2020: Victoria Graf's EEG project was entered as "behavioral and social sciences" (https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/STS/2020/Program-Books/Finalist.pdf).
  - 2022: Benjamin Choi's EEG prosthesis was "bioengineering" (https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/STS/2022/Program-Books/Finalist.pdf).
  - 2024: Alexis Li's fMRI pipeline was "neuroscience" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2024-finalists/).
  - 2026: Edward Kang's RetinaMind was "neuroscience" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2026-sts-winners/).
  - 2025: Siddharth Nirgudkar's Alzheimer's diagnosis model was "computational biology and bioinformatics" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2025-finalists/).
  - 2026: Leon Wang's Alzheimer's drug repurposing was "neuroscience" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2026-sts-winners/).
  - 2026: Jashvi Desai's Long COVID MRI project was "medicine and health" (https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2026-finalists/).
