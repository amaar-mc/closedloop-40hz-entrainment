# Presentation Flow: Reading Judges & Pacing

## The Core Principle

CSEF is a conversation, not a speech. Judges have read your work. They don't want a recitation. They want to see that you UNDERSTAND it.

Your job is not to convey information (they have it). Your job is to demonstrate comprehension, show scientific maturity, and be memorable enough that they argue for you in the caucus.

---

## How to Read Which Script to Use

**Judge walks up and says "Tell me about your project":**
- Use the 60-second opening. Stop at about 45-50 seconds. Let them jump in.
- If they don't interrupt, you land the result and say "What would you like to dig into?"

**Judge walks up and says "I read your presentation -- interesting project":**
- They don't want a walkthrough. They want to talk.
- Say: "Thanks -- what caught your attention?" or "Happy to go deep on any part of it."
- Let THEM pick the topic. Answer with depth.

**Judge walks up and immediately asks a specific question:**
- Answer it. Don't try to redirect to your prepared opening.
- After answering, you can offer: "Would you like me give a quick overview, or do you have more questions?"

**Judge seems confused or hasn't read your work:**
- Some judges may not have had time to read everyone's presentation.
- Use the 2-minute core. Keep it accessible.

## Pacing Within an Interview

A typical 10-minute interview:
- **0:00-1:30:** Your opening (60s or 2min depending on their cue)
- **1:30-8:00:** Back-and-forth Q&A. They ask, you answer. This is the heart of it.
- **8:00-9:30:** Deeper follow-up on whatever topic they're most interested in
- **9:30-10:00:** Closing. "Any other questions? Thanks for the conversation."

Don't try to cover everything. Better to go deep on 3 topics than shallow on 10.

## Depth Calibration

**Signs they want MORE depth:**
- Leaning in
- Asking follow-up questions ("How exactly?" "Can you elaborate?")
- Nodding while you speak
- Taking notes
- Referencing specific parts of your poster or presentation
--> Go deeper. Use Level 2 or 3 answers. Give the exact numbers. Reference papers.

**Signs they want LESS depth:**
- Looking away or at other displays
- Saying "OK" or "Right" quickly to move you along
- Checking the time
- Glazed expression
- Not asking follow-ups
--> Simplify. Use analogies. Hit the key result and move on. Don't lecture.

**Signs they're testing you (not confused -- probing):**
- Asking "Why?" repeatedly
- Playing devil's advocate ("But couldn't you just...")
- Asking you to explain a term you used
- Saying "I'm not sure I follow" (might be genuine confusion or a test)
--> Stay calm. Explain clearly. If they're testing, they'll respect thoroughness. If genuinely confused, they'll appreciate patience.

## The Product Demo (Muse 2 + Laptop)

When to bring it up:
- When asked about future directions or clinical use
- When asked "could this actually help patients?"
- When asked about the hardware
- NOT during your opening (it's a prop, not the main act)

How to use it:
1. Pick up the Muse 2. "This is the headset -- about $200, consumer-grade EEG."
2. Show the headphones. "Standard headphones deliver the 40 Hz tones."
3. Turn the laptop to face them. "This is the caregiver app running in real time with simulated EEG. You can see the controller making decisions -- when to stimulate, when to rest."
4. Point to the PAC trace on screen. "That's phase-amplitude coupling updating live."
5. "The whole system -- headset, headphones, laptop -- costs under $250."

Keep it to 30-60 seconds. Don't let the demo take over the interview.

## Forbidden Phrases

Never say:
- "Basically..." (sounds like you're dumbing it down)
- "It's kind of like..." (be direct about what it IS)
- "To be honest..." (implies you weren't being honest before)
- "That's a great question" (judges see through this instantly)
- "What we did was..." (say "I" -- this is individual work)
- "I leveraged / harnessed / utilized" (AI voice)
- "Cutting-edge / state-of-the-art / groundbreaking" (marketing speak)

Instead:
- Just answer directly
- "I found that..." / "The data shows..." / "When I tested this..."
- Keep it first-person, past tense, specific
