# Day-of Checklist: CSEF 2026

## What to Bring

### Must Have

- [ ] Printed poster (48x64 in) -- already done
- [ ] Printed 13-page project presentation (judges may ask to see it at your table)
- [ ] Lab notebook (even if not mandatory -- demonstrates rigor)
- [ ] Laptop -- FULLY CHARGED, have the HuggingFace demo ready to show
- [ ] Muse 2 headset + headphones (physical product demo)
- [ ] Charger / portable battery (NO AC power at displays)
- [ ] This numbers sheet printed (review between interviews)
- [ ] Water bottle
- [ ] Snacks (protein bars, not candy)

### Should Have

- [ ] Extra printed copy of the poster content (in case a judge wants to read something closely)
- [ ] Sticky notes flagging key pages in your lab notebook
- [ ] Pen (judges may ask you to sketch something)
- [ ] Backup laptop charger or power bank

### Don't Bring

- QR codes (prohibited on display)
- Business cards or handouts (distribution to judges is prohibited)
- Previous fair awards or medals
- Anything that requires AC power to function

## Setup (Saturday April 11)

1. Arrive early. Scope out your location.
2. Mount poster on the board. Make sure it's straight and secure.
3. Arrange table: laptop (charged, demo loaded), Muse 2 + headphones, notebook with sticky tabs, printed presentation.
4. Check that the laptop demo works. Test the HuggingFace app loads offline if wifi is unreliable.
5. Take a photo of your setup for reference.
6. Walk the hall. Look at competitors in your category. Don't psych yourself out -- just get a feel for the room.

## Judging Day (Sunday April 12)

### Pre-Judging

- Eat a real breakfast. Protein + complex carbs.
- Arrive at least 30 minutes before judging starts.
- Check your display. Nothing shifted overnight.
- Boot laptop, load demo, verify it works.
- Do a 60-second dry run of your opening. Out loud, quietly.
- Review the numbers sheet one more time.
- Use the bathroom before judging starts.

### During Judging (~12:45-4:30 PM, per CSEF 2026 schedule)

- Stand when a judge approaches. Don't be sitting when they arrive.
- Greet them warmly. Shake hands if offered.
- Let them lead. Some will say "tell me about it." Others will jump to questions.
- Between interviews: sip water, reset posture, take a breath.
- Don't check your phone between interviews. Stay present and ready.
- If no judge is at your booth, review your danger zones mentally.

### During the Break

- Sit down. Rest your legs and voice.
- Eat your snack.
- Don't worry about which judges you've seen or haven't.
- Quick mental review of any question you stumbled on -- prepare a better answer.

### After Judging

- Stay at your display until officially dismissed.
- If judges come by informally after judging ends, still be engaged.
- Break down your display when instructed.

## What to Wear

Business casual. Not a suit. Not a hoodie.

- Button-down or nice polo. Dark pants or khakis. Clean shoes.
- Comfortable enough to stand for 4+ hours.
- No logos, no distracting patterns.
- Look like you take this seriously but aren't performing.

## Mental Prep

- You've done the work. 35 patients. 5 seeds. 8 architectures. Every number is real.
- The judges are interested -- they volunteered to be here. They want to learn about your project.
- A tough question is a compliment. It means they're engaged enough to probe.
- You don't need to be perfect. You need to be genuine, knowledgeable, and memorable.
- One sentence to retell: "He predicts when a patient's brain will lose its response to therapy, five seconds before it happens, and it worked on all 35 patients."
