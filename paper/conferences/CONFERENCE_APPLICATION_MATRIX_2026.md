# Conference Application Matrix, Summer 2026

**Prepared:** 2026-06-14  
**Applicant:** 17-year-old rising high-school senior in San Jose, California  
**Project:** retrospective EEG/PAC forecasting and offline replay for adaptive 40 Hz auditory-stimulation research  
**Current submission asset:** MIT URTC-format five-page manuscript plus DOCX/text-proof PDF in
`paper/conferences/mit_urtc_2026/draft/`.

## Executive Status

The research is deep enough for high-school research conferences, student symposia, poster tracks,
and MIT URTC poster/lightning. It is also technically competitive for MIT URTC paper review, but the
paper track remains administratively blocked unless there is a real qualifying university
relationship from the time this work was done.

The current documented record says the project was independent, with no institutional lab or
university mentor. On that record:

- MIT URTC paper track: do not submit unless eligibility changes truthfully.
- MIT URTC poster/lightning: viable and strong.
- Professional biomedical/neuroengineering venues: possible, but require tighter framing and,
  for full-paper tracks, additional robustness work.
- High-school research competitions: very strong fit if presented as retrospective computational
  neuroscience / biomedical signal-processing research, not clinical therapy.

| JSHS / STS / AAN-style high-school research   | Enough to submit, but improve narrative                   | These reward original high-school research and clear scientific maturity. Emphasize the failure-mode audit.                        |
| IEEE SPMB paper/abstract                      | Close, but should add robustness if submitting full paper | Strong topic fit; professional audience will notice the single fixed split, single-seed backward-looking benchmark, and Ridge tie. |
| SfN / BCI Meeting / ICASSP-style reach venues | Not enough as-is for a full research paper                | Needs stronger neuroscience validation, repeated participant splits, streaming-compatible PAC, and feature ablations.              |

Highest-value depth upgrades before serious paper venues:

1. Repeat the backward-looking PAC stress test across multiple seeds and repeated participant splits.
2. Add PAC-only, stimulation-context-only, and combined-feature ablations under the backward-looking target.
3. Report per-participant or grouped-CV variability.
4. Add a transition-specific evaluation, because event-summary labels repeat heavily.
5. Keep all claims retrospective: no therapeutic, clinical, or deployed closed-loop claims.

## College-Application Positioning

Best honest phrasing if no paper-track publication is secured yet:

> Developed an independent retrospective EEG/ML study on PAC forecasting for adaptive 40 Hz auditory
> stimulation; built a four-page IEEE-style manuscript and submitted/presented the work through
> high-school-eligible research venues, with a focus on leakage audits and realistic target-definition
> stress testing.

If MIT URTC poster/lightning is submitted:

> Submitted to MIT IEEE URTC poster/lightning track; paper-track submission withheld unless university
> eligibility is satisfied.

If SPMB or BMES is submitted:

> Submitted computational neuroscience / biomedical signal-processing abstract to [venue], emphasizing
> retrospective EEG forecasting, baseline comparisons, and target-availability auditing.

Avoid:

- "published in IEEE" unless the paper is accepted, presented, and actually enters proceedings.
- "closed-loop therapy" or "clinical validation."
- listing a mentor, lab, AI Club, or university affiliation unless the relationship is real and specific to this work.
