# Background Literature and Research Gap

## 1. Gamma Oscillations and Alzheimer's Disease

Gamma oscillations (30-100 Hz) are the highest-frequency brain rhythms and are essential for memory encoding, sensory binding, attention, and long-range cortical communication. They are generated by the Pyramidal-Interneuron Network Gamma (PING) mechanism: fast-spiking parvalbumin-positive (PV) inhibitory interneurons fire at gamma frequency and synchronize pyramidal neuron activity into coherent oscillatory patterns.

Alzheimer's disease disrupts this machinery. Reduced gamma power and impaired cross-regional gamma synchronization are observed early in disease progression — in some transgenic mouse models, before amyloid plaques are detectable. The disruption is not merely correlative: network dysfunction is now understood to be a proximate cause of cognitive impairment, potentially preceding protein aggregation rather than following it. Approximately 20-40% of AD patients also exhibit epileptiform activity, reflecting the broader network hyperexcitability that accompanies gamma degradation.

## 2. Phase-Amplitude Coupling as a Biomarker

Phase-amplitude coupling (PAC) quantifies how the amplitude of a high-frequency oscillation (here, gamma at 38-42 Hz) is modulated by the phase of a low-frequency oscillation (here, theta at 4-8 Hz). Theta-gamma PAC is the neural mechanism by which the brain organizes information for working memory: each theta cycle (125-250 ms) provides a temporal window in which gamma bursts encode individual items, and the number of gamma cycles per theta cycle determines working memory capacity.

PAC is measured using the Modulation Index (MI), introduced by Tort et al. (2010, J. Neurophysiology). The MI is derived from the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the observed amplitude distribution across phase bins and a uniform distribution (which would indicate no coupling). MI ranges from 0 (no coupling) to 1 (perfect coupling); typical values in healthy adults are 0.005-0.025.

Multiple studies establish PAC as a sensitive biomarker for Alzheimer's:

- **Theta-gamma coupling is the strongest predictor of working memory performance** in AD, MCI, and healthy controls (standardized beta = 0.693, p < 0.001), with AD patients showing the lowest coupling (2018).
- **Altered PAC is among the earliest EEG signatures of MCI**, preceding clinical diagnosis and serving as a potential screening biomarker (2020).
- **Reduced gamma PAC in the parahippocampal cortex** correlates with network hyperexcitability, and patients with epileptiform activity show the greatest PAC reductions (2024).

This makes PAC the natural target for optimizing entrainment therapy: high PAC indicates effective coupling; low PAC indicates a therapeutic window where stimulation is most needed.

## 3. 40 Hz Entrainment Therapy

### Foundational Evidence

Iaccarino et al. (2016, Nature) demonstrated that optogenetic 40 Hz stimulation of PV interneurons in AD model mice reduced amyloid-beta levels by 40-50% and upregulated genes associated with microglial phagocytosis. This landmark finding established that external gamma-frequency stimulation could modify disease pathology through immune-mediated clearance.

### Mechanisms of Action

Subsequent research identified three interconnected therapeutic pathways:

1. **Microglial activation and phagocytosis.** 40 Hz stimulation upregulates cytokines (IL-6, IL-4, M-CSF) that transform microglia into a phagocytic state, enabling them to engulf and clear amyloid-beta plaques.

2. **Glymphatic clearance.** Combined audiovisual 40 Hz stimulation promotes cerebrospinal fluid influx and interstitial fluid efflux, facilitating waste clearance through the brain's drainage system. Blocking the glymphatic pathway abolishes the amyloid removal effect (Nature, 2024).

3. **Network restoration.** Entrainment enhances functional connectivity in the default mode network (DMN) and frontoparietal networks, restoring connectivity patterns toward those observed in healthy controls (Lahijanian et al., 2024, Scientific Reports).

### Translational Evidence

- **Aged primate model:** Long-term 40 Hz auditory stimulation (5+ weeks) in rhesus monkeys elevated CSF amyloid-beta levels, indicating mobilization and clearance — the first long-term non-human primate evidence (PNAS, 2025).
- **Human clinical trials:** Cognito Therapeutics' Phase II trial showed slowed brain atrophy and preserved cognitive function, with plasma pTau217 reductions of 19-47% in responders. Strong EEG entrainment was retained in responders (Chan et al., 2025, Alzheimer's & Dementia).

## 4. The Individual Variability Problem

Despite consistent group-level results, individual responses to 40 Hz entrainment vary dramatically:

- In clinical trials, approximately 23 out of 33 participants showed robust entrainment while 10 showed minimal response — a 30% non-responder rate.
- Baseline neural states, sensory processing abilities, disease severity, and brain volume (particularly right hippocampal volume) all predict response magnitude.
- Among responders, trajectories diverge over time: some patients habituate (declining PAC during sustained stimulation), while others maintain or increase coupling.

This variability is not noise — it reflects genuine individual differences in neural architecture, disease progression, and sensory processing. Current fixed-schedule protocols (40s ON / 20s OFF, identical for all patients) cannot accommodate it.

## 5. Closed-Loop Neuromodulation

The concept of adaptive neuromodulation is well-established in other domains:

- **Closed-loop deep brain stimulation (DBS)** for Parkinson's disease reduces side effects, slows habituation, and extends device battery life compared to continuous stimulation (2019).
- **Deep learning model predictive control for DBS** outperforms linear MPC by 10% and PI controllers by 20% while being 5% more energy-efficient (2025).
- **Real-time EEG prediction** of cortical excitability (the PRIME framework) has demonstrated that end-to-end deep learning can predict brain states from raw EEG and automatically identify individualized biomarkers (2025).

However, no existing system applies closed-loop adaptive control to 40 Hz gamma entrainment therapy for Alzheimer's disease.

## 6. The Research Gap

The literature converges on a clear gap:

| What exists | What is missing |
|-------------|-----------------|
| 40 Hz entrainment reduces amyloid and restores connectivity | Stimulation timing is not optimized to individual brain states |
| PAC is a validated, sensitive biomarker for AD and entrainment quality | No system predicts future PAC to enable proactive intervention |
| 30% of patients are non-responders; 50% habituate over time | Fixed schedules cannot adapt to individual response trajectories |
| Closed-loop neuromodulation improves outcomes in DBS | No closed-loop system exists for 40 Hz entrainment |
| Deep learning can predict brain states from EEG in real time | This capability has not been applied to PAC forecasting for entrainment |

This project addresses the gap by building a predictive closed-loop system that forecasts PAC 5-10 seconds ahead and uses those predictions to deliver stimulation only when it is most needed — personalized to each patient's neural dynamics.
